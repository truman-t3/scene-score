# Translation grammar

Choose marks from the photographed scene. This is a routing guide, not a menu to combine wholesale.

## Score roles

- **Pulse:** make a photographed repetition or interval visible as measured bands, clusters, or marks. It carries rhythm, not decoration.
- **Phrase:** make one photographed direction, projection, or value shift visible as a continuous line, field, or offset. It carries movement.
- **Rest:** enlarge a factual quiet zone into breathing space. It carries tempo by withholding detail.
- **Accent:** use one small source color or event as a brief change of emphasis.

Choose two or three roles. Do not draw notes, staffs, or music symbols unless the user explicitly requests them.

## Difference gate

Before accepting an image, name two structural changes relative to the source. Each change must be a scene-derived interval, field, line, mass, or boundary that remains obvious when the image is viewed small. Paper texture, color grading, haze, and a near-identical crop do not satisfy the gate.

## Natural field scores

For landscapes, gardens, water, and other organic scenes, translate changing conditions rather than forcing architectural bars or a single flat silhouette:

- Let **pulse** come from density shifts: clustered leaves, reeds, ripples, rock, snow, or cloud. Use selected gaps, thinning, and overlap rather than equal marks.
- Let **phrase** come from a long gradual force: horizon, boat wake, wind, slope, tide, or changing light. It may become a translucent stratum or offset field, not necessarily a line.
- Let **rest** come from factual fog, sky, water, or open ground. Its edges should breathe or dissolve, not become an arbitrary empty panel.
- Make one decisive natural threshold visible, such as canopy-to-fog, reed-to-water, or near-to-far. It should be clear at thumbnail size while retaining organic variation.

Avoid a full flat cut-paper canopy, rows of identical reeds, or a nearly unchanged landscape with only grain and atmosphere.

| Observed force | Faithful graphic translation | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|
| Walking route, vehicle route, gaze | one continuous route line, a measured broken path, or a single directional offset | the source contains no credible direction |
| Rain, wind, falling light | sparse directional threads with source-matched angle and density | it becomes a full-page texture |
| Window light, reflection, shadow | a translucent band, projected plane, or value shift following the actual light geometry | a default circle replaces the actual light source |
| Horizon, water, shelves, crosswalk | horizontal intervals with source-based widths and spacing | the scene lacks a horizontal rhythm |
| Tree, reed, fabric, crowd | a variable-density rhythm that follows growth, fold, or clustering | it reads as random barcode decoration |
| Circular lamp, sun, vessel, wheel | a restrained disc or ring tied to its real source | there is no circular source or visual force |
| Repeated façades, windows, steps | modular blocks or a measured field with selected omissions | it becomes a decorative grid |
| Contrast between near and far | one anchored photographic area against a simplified field | the simplified field erases scene identity |

## Palette rules

- Derive the field from the scene's quiet zone: fog, wall, water, tabletop, sky, or shadow. Do not default to ivory.
- Derive the accent from a small high-salience source color: a shop light, leaf, reflective object, garment, or sign-free surface.
- Keep ink close to the source's darkest stable color, not necessarily black.
- Use no more than one accent unless the source itself demands a color relationship.

## Composition rules

- In amplified score mode, start with one **hero geometry** that makes the thumbnail memorable. Derive its role from the source, then allow it to be larger and simpler than its literal origin: warm shop glow can become an amber disc; a crossing can become route bands; a perfume bottle and window shaft can become a translucent upright column; a tree canopy can become one gold mass; a lake can become a broad horizontal field.
- A hero geometry is a visual role, not a stock shape. Change its silhouette, placement, scale, and palette with the source. If the same shape would work unchanged for three unrelated images, it is too generic.
- Do not treat the photographic anchor as a sticker. Let one observed edge cross the boundary: a road can continue into an ink band, a window shaft can continue into a translucent plane, a horizon can continue into a fog field, or a bottle axis can rise into a graphic column. The boundary may be interrupted or absorbed where this connection is strongest.
- In structural rebuild mode, preserve one photographic anchor at roughly 25–45% of the frame. Re-author the remainder as a source-derived field; the result must be visibly more than a photo overlay.
- Let the field's edge grow from the source: a crossing may bend along its route; a window-light field may follow its projection; a water field may follow its horizon; a tree field may follow its trunk and canopy mass.
- The original scene's open area can become quiet field, but do not invent empty space by arbitrarily cropping away the subject.
- One score layer can overlap the photo when it clarifies a relationship. Multiple unrelated overlays usually become collage.
